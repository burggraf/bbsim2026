# Batter–pitcher probability design note (research, not implemented)

**2026-10-10; P-01/A-01 planning only.** No probability model, player rating, fitted prior or simulation has been implemented. G0 remains pending and E-01/P-01 must not begin under the current gate. The maintainer requested a Bayesian/shrunk probability for **every legal outcome for every player**, including events absent from that player's sample; no sampled legal category should receive an empirical zero merely because its observed count was zero. This document records the research and intended tests, **not** a claim that any formula yields a known “true probability.”

## Research and proposed comparisons

- The [SABR generalized log5 derivation](https://sabr.org/journal/article/matchup-probabilities-in-major-league-baseball/) extends binary batter/pitcher/league log5 to mutually exclusive outcomes by normalizing weights `w_k = b_k × p_k / ℓ_k` so `P(k|b,p) = w_k / Σ_j w_j`. It used 2012 Retrosheet PA data for seven coarse outcomes and restricted the player sample to hitters/pitchers with at least 502 opportunities. It is a sensible **baseline**, not a proven universal best model for sparse 2019 players, every event type or runner/fielding transitions. Independent binary rolls for each outcome would not form one coherent distribution.
- [Doo & Kim (2018), PLOS ONE](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0204874) studied a **Bayesian hierarchical log5** model with prior information from ordinary log5; it reported better predictive performance than the compared standard/generalized log5 methods in its real-data example. Its principal target was **whether the batter reaches base**, a binary event—not a complete PA outcome tree or proof of the best simulator.
- [Powers, Hastie & Tibshirani, *Statistical Modelling* (2018)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.10272) studied **regularized multinomial** batter–pitcher outcome estimates that share information among categories and account for opponent effects. This is a useful higher-complexity challenger, not automatically preferable on our sample or a Bayesian method merely because it is regularized.

**Proposed P-01 baseline:** estimate a positive league/era distribution for mutually exclusive **terminal PA categories** and posterior player distributions with positive, league-centered priors. For a simple Dirichlet–multinomial component, `E[θ_player,k | data] = (n_player,k + α_player,k) / (N_player + Σ_j α_player,j)` with `α_player,k = κ_player ℓ_k > 0` for every **legal** category, `κ_player > 0`, and a positive broader prior if a rare category is absent even at league level. Thus `n_player,k = 0` still yields a positive posterior predictive estimate, with uncertainty and shrinkage depending on exposure and prior strength. Use those estimates in a normalized generalized-log5 matchup baseline, or test a multinomial logit with league baseline plus shrunk batter and pitcher effects. Simple separate player marginals can confound opponent strength, park and defense; a joint hierarchical/regularized model is a candidate improvement, not an automatic winner. Fit/choose prior strength from appropriate training data, preserve snapshot/rules/model versions and report opportunities; do **not** hardcode `0.00000001` as an uncalibrated pseudo-probability. A very rare event may have a tiny positive probability, but its magnitude should have a defensible prior and sensitivity check.

**Legal/contextual sample space:** a player has positive probability for each feasible category when eligible, but the game **must** assign exactly zero to structurally impossible events (for example, stealing an unoccupied base). IBB is a manager policy choice, not an unconditional batter PA tendency. Runner-only/pitch events, advancement, sacrifices and later contact→fielding effects have conditional opportunity models rather than being mixed into a flat unconditional PA list. The Phase-1 final PA distribution includes hits/errors; Phase-2 must generate contact and fielding **before** final hit/out/error, not overturn an already chosen hit with an independent defense roll. For each legal context the probabilities are finite, strictly positive, normalized to one and sampled once; sparse players revert toward pooled league/context estimates, never an empirical zero. Test those invariants and rare-category nonzero behavior explicitly.

**Model selection is deferred, not guessed:** before fitting P-01, preregister training/held-out or as-of-date splits, outcome taxonomy, opportunity filters, smoothing/prior selection and conditional calibration/log-loss (plus baseline tolerances). Compare the simple smoothed league-only model, normalized generalized log5 and hierarchical/regularized multinomial alternatives on data not used to tune them, including sparse players and rare categories. Report calibration, log loss, player opportunity sizes, uncertainty and mechanism checks—not only league aggregate hits/runs. Same-2019 ratings are **hindsight reconstruction**, not evidence of predictive ability. A more elaborate Bayesian model is not “best” without that comparison.
